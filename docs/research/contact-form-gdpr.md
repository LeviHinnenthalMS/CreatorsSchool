# GDPR and the Creators School contact form

Research date: 2026-07-27

## Short answer

A mandatory consent checkbox is generally **not required** merely to receive and answer an ordinary contact-form inquiry. The form processing still needs a lawful basis, but consent is only one of the lawful bases in GDPR Article 6.

The site **should provide a clearly visible link to the relevant privacy information at the form**, for example immediately below the message field or beside the submit button. This is the clearest way to make the Article 13 information available when the data are collected. The link can be a notice; users do not need to tick a box confirming that they have read it.

Suggested German copy:

> Informationen zur Verarbeitung deiner Angaben findest du in unserer [Datenschutzerklärung].

## Legal basis for answering inquiries

- **Article 6(1)(b):** If a person asks about booking, enrolment, pricing, or another possible contract, processing the submitted details may be necessary to take pre-contractual steps at that person's request.
- **Article 6(1)(f):** Other ordinary inquiries may instead be handled on the basis of the controller's legitimate interest in receiving and answering communications, provided the processing is necessary and the person's interests, rights, and freedoms do not override that interest.
- **Article 6(1)(a):** Consent is an alternative lawful basis, not a universal prerequisite for processing personal data.

The correct basis depends on the inquiry and must match what the site's privacy notice says. See the official [GDPR text, Article 6](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/2016-05-04/eng) and the EDPB's [guidance on lawful processing](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/be-compliant/process-personal-data-lawfully_en).

## Why a forced consent checkbox can be the wrong approach

Consent must be freely given, specific, informed, unambiguous, and withdrawable. Article 7(4) also requires particular attention where providing a service is conditional on consent to processing that is not necessary for the service.

The EDPB explains that if an organisation must process the information and cannot genuinely allow withdrawal without consequences, this indicates that consent is not the appropriate legal basis. Requiring “consent” to the processing that is inherently needed to answer the person's own message can therefore misstate the legal basis and create unnecessary consent-withdrawal obligations.

Sources:

- [GDPR, Articles 4(11) and 7](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/2016-05-04/eng)
- [EDPB Guidelines 05/2020 on consent](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/guideline/guidelines-052020-on-consent-under-regulation-2016679_en)
- [BfDI guidance on consent](https://www.bfdi.bund.de/DE/Buerger/Inhalte/Allgemein/Datenschutz/Einwilligung.html)

If the form also offers an optional, separate purpose such as newsletter or promotional emails, that purpose should have its own unchecked, optional consent box. It must not be bundled into submission of the ordinary inquiry.

## Privacy notice and link

GDPR Articles 12(1) and 13 require privacy information to be concise, transparent, intelligible, easily accessible, and supplied when personal data are obtained. The information includes, among other things:

- the controller and contact details;
- purposes and legal basis;
- the legitimate interests relied upon, if Article 6(1)(f) is used;
- recipients or categories of recipients;
- any relevant international-transfer information;
- retention period or criteria;
- applicable data-subject rights and the right to complain to a supervisory authority;
- whether providing the data is required and the consequences of not providing it.

An adjacent link to the contact-form section of the Datenschutz page is a sensible layered approach. A footer link may help make the notice generally accessible, but placing a contextual link directly at the form makes compliance at the point of collection clearer.

The [LfD Niedersachsen website guidance](https://www.lfd.niedersachsen.de/faq/website_betreiber/faq-webseiten-datenschutzkonform-betreiben-166238.html) says a privacy declaration must be easy to find and clearly recognisable, identify the purposes and legal bases, and cover features through which users directly submit personal data, including contact forms. The [LDI NRW model notice for non-public websites](https://www.ldi.nrw.de/system/files/media/document/file/muster-fur-datenschutzhinweise-fur-websites.pdf) says privacy information must be available when data are collected and linked so it is directly reachable with one click from every page.

## Exceptions and cautions

- If a free-text field is intended to collect Article 9 special-category data, Article 6 alone is not enough; an Article 9(2) exception is also required. The safer default is to avoid requesting such data and tell users not to submit sensitive information unless the service genuinely requires it.
- The form and its processors, recipients, retention practice, and any third-country transfers must match the Datenschutz text.
- Contact-form transmission should use current HTTPS encryption. The [LfD Niedersachsen guidance](https://www.lfd.niedersachsen.de/faq/website_betreiber/faq-webseiten-datenschutzkonform-betreiben-166238.html) expressly calls for secure encrypted transmission of data entered on websites.

This note is a focused GDPR assessment, not formal legal advice or a complete review of the form and privacy notice.
